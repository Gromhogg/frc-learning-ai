GENERAL KNOWLEDGE OF FRC (FIRST ROBOTICS COMPETITION)
======================================================

This file is the source of truth the AI mentor uses when assessing students on
general FRC knowledge. Keep it factual, current, and team-agnostic where
possible. Game-specific facts (this year's scoring, field elements, etc.) age
quickly — note that explicitly so questions remain evergreen.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. WHAT FRC IS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- FRC stands for FIRST Robotics Competition.
- FIRST = For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology, founded
  by Dean Kamen and Woodie Flowers in 1989.
- FRC is the flagship high-school program in the FIRST family, which also
  includes FLL (Lego League, ages 4–16), FTC (Tech Challenge, ages 12–18),
  and FIRST in Show (formerly Jr.FLL).
- Teams build a roughly 125-pound (≈57 kg) robot in 6–8 weeks each year to
  compete in a new game revealed at Kickoff.
- Teams are identified by a permanent number (e.g. "Team 254", "Team 1678",
  "Team 972"). Lower numbers were founded earlier.
- The season culminates at the FIRST Championship (a single combined event;
  in some years it has been split between Houston and Detroit).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. SEASON STRUCTURE (THE FRC CALENDAR)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- KICKOFF (first Saturday of January): the new game and full Game Manual are
  released. The first 24 hours are "Kickoff Day" — teams read the manual,
  watch the game animation, and start prototyping.
- BUILD SEASON (≈6–8 weeks, January–February): design, prototype, fabricate,
  wire, program, and practice. Subdivides into:
    * Strategy & game analysis
    * Prototyping
    * CAD freeze
    * Manufacturing
    * Assembly
    * Wiring & pneumatics
    * Programming & autonomous tuning
    * Driver practice
- "Stop Build Day" was retired in 2020; teams now have an unbagged robot and
  can iterate throughout the season under withholding-allowance rules.
- COMPETITION SEASON (March–April): District events or Regional events.
- CHAMPIONSHIP (mid–late April).
- OFF-SEASON (May–December): training, recruitment, outreach, off-season
  events (e.g. IRI, Chezy Champs, Battlecry).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. GAME STRUCTURE (EVERGREEN CONCEPTS)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A match is 2 minutes 30 seconds long:
- AUTONOMOUS period: first 15 seconds. No driver input — robot runs pre-
  programmed routines using sensors. Worth bonus points and ranking-point
  contributions.
- TELEOP period: remaining 2 minutes 15 seconds. Drivers control robots from
  behind a glass driver station.
- ENDGAME: last ~20–30 seconds of teleop. Usually has bonus mechanics —
  climbing a chain/bar, parking in a zone, balancing on a charging station,
  etc., depending on the year's game.

Matches are 3-vs-3 alliances: each match has a RED alliance and a BLUE
alliance, three robots per side, on a ~27' × 54' carpeted field.

Scoring depends on the year (game pieces, point values, multipliers). The
AI should ask about evergreen concepts (autonomous, teleop, endgame,
alliances, ranking points) rather than current scoring tables, which change
annually.

RANKING POINTS (RPs): teams earn RPs in qualification matches in addition
to win/loss/tie. Two RPs are always given for a Win (1 for a Tie). The other
RPs change each year (often for hitting a scoring threshold and a "co-op"
or sustainability objective).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. EVENT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- QUALIFICATION ("quals"): ~8–12 round-robin matches per team. Pseudo-random
  alliance partners. Teams accumulate ranking points; the top 8 advance as
  alliance captains.
- ALLIANCE SELECTION: top 8 teams pick partners in a snake-draft (1 picks
  first, then 8 picks, then 8 picks again, etc.) until 8 alliances of 3 are
  formed.
- PLAYOFFS: as of 2023, FRC uses a double-elimination bracket of 8 alliances
  (was best-of-3 single-elimination before 2023). Matches are best-of-1 in
  the bracket; finals are best-of-3.
- DISTRICT MODEL (some regions: New England, FMA, FIM, ONT, ISR, IN, CHS,
  PCH, PNW, NC, TX): teams play 2 District Events, earn points, top teams
  advance to District Championship, then to Worlds.
- REGIONAL MODEL (everywhere else): teams play 1 Regional; winners get
  Worlds slots.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. ROBOT — CORE RULES & INSPECTION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- WEIGHT LIMIT: 125 lb (about 57 kg) excluding battery and bumpers.
- FRAME PERIMETER: the smallest convex polygon that contains the robot
  at the start of the match, measured at standing height. Typical limit
  is 120" perimeter (~28" × 32" robot), but the year's game can change it.
- HEIGHT LIMIT: usually 4'–4'6" starting height, with extension allowed
  during the match up to a year-specific cap.
- BUMPERS are mandatory. Made of pool noodles + fabric, attached around
  the frame perimeter at a specific height (typically 0–7.5" off the
  carpet). Must be RED or BLUE depending on which alliance the robot is
  on, with team number visible on both colors.
- ROBOT INSPECTION at each event checks weight, dimensions, bumper rules,
  electrical wiring, breakers, battery security, and the "kill switch"
  (a robot signal light, RSL, that must blink solid orange/yellow when
  enabled).
- BATTERY: a single 12V sealed lead-acid (SLA) battery, ~17 Ah. Brand
  examples: MK ES17-12, EnerSys NP18-12. Each robot has multiple
  batteries that rotate during a competition day.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. ELECTRICAL SYSTEM
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CORE COMPONENTS (every legal FRC robot has these):
- BATTERY (12 V SLA) → wired through the 120 A main breaker → to the
  Power Distribution Hub (PDH, current REV product) or older Power
  Distribution Panel (PDP, CTRE).
- PDH/PDP: distributes power through individual breakers to motor
  controllers, the radio (via VRM/REV PoE radio adapter), and the
  RoboRIO.
- RoboRIO: the FRC robot's primary computer, runs robot code. NI/REV
  product. Communicates with motor controllers over CAN, PWM, or DIO.
- VRM (Voltage Regulator Module) or RPM (Radio Power Module): steps
  battery voltage down to clean 5 V or 12 V outputs for the radio and
  small electronics.
- RADIO: OM5P-AC (legacy) or VH-109 (current). Bridges the field
  Wi-Fi/wired network to the RoboRIO.
- RSL: Robot Signal Light. Must be visible and blink when robot is
  enabled, solid when disabled. Required by inspection.

BREAKERS:
- 120 A main breaker between battery (+) and PDH.
- 40 A "Snap-Action" breakers (MX-5 style) for motor circuits.
- 20 A or 30 A for smaller circuits.
- 10 A "Wago" / blade breakers for low-current things like Spark MAX
  servos (rarely needed).

WIRE GAUGE (RULES OF THUMB):
- Battery to PDH: 6 AWG.
- High-current motor branches (40 A circuits, e.g. NEO/Falcon): 10 AWG.
- 20 A or 30 A branches: 12 AWG.
- Low-current / signal / sensor: 18–22 AWG.
- Always crimp Anderson SB50 connectors on battery leads; never solder
  battery terminals to wire directly.

CAN BUS:
- Most modern motor controllers (Talon FX/Kraken, Spark MAX/Flex,
  Talon SRX, Victor SPX), the PDH, the Pigeon IMU, and CANcoders sit
  on a single CAN bus terminated at the PDH and the RoboRIO.
- CAN runs as a daisy-chain (yellow = CAN-H, green = CAN-L). Two 120-Ω
  terminating resistors form the bus — one is built into the RoboRIO,
  one into the PDH. Don't add extra terminators.
- Each device on CAN has a unique CAN ID (0–62). Conflicting IDs is a
  classic and time-wasting bug.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. MOTORS & MOTOR CONTROLLERS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

COMMON BRUSHLESS MOTORS (modern FRC, all CAN-controlled):
- KRAKEN X60 / KRAKEN X44 (CTRE, integrated TalonFX). Most powerful
  legal motor; ~7.09 N·m stall on the X60.
- NEO V1.1 / NEO Vortex (REV, used with Spark MAX or Spark Flex). Very
  common drivetrain choice; integrated hall-effect encoder.
- FALCON 500 (CTRE, integrated TalonFX). Predecessor to Kraken; still
  legal and widespread.

BRUSHED LEGACY MOTORS (becoming rarer but legal):
- CIM, MiniCIM, BAG, 775pro, RedLine, AndyMark 9015. Use Spark MAX,
  Talon SRX, Victor SPX, or Spark (PWM) controllers.

CONTROLLERS — KEY PAIRINGS:
- TalonFX is INTEGRATED into Kraken/Falcon (no separate controller).
- Spark MAX = REV brushed/brushless controller (typically for NEO).
- Spark Flex = REV controller for NEO Vortex.
- Talon SRX, Victor SPX = CTRE brushed legacy controllers.
- Spark (Rev), VictorSP, TalonSR = PWM-only legacy.

SERVOS: PWM hobby servos, typically 5 V from the PDH 5 V rail or VRM.

ABSOLUTE/INCREMENTAL ENCODERS:
- CANcoder (CTRE), Cancoder, Through-Bore Encoder (REV), SRX Mag Encoder.
- Use absolute encoders on swerve modules and any joint that needs to
  know its position at boot.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. PNEUMATICS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- Pneumatics are OPTIONAL. Many modern designs skip them entirely.
- COMPRESSOR (12 V, e.g. VIAIR 90C / Thomas 405) charges the system.
- WORKING PRESSURE: 60 psi (regulated downstream).
- STORAGE PRESSURE: max 120 psi (unregulated tank side).
- Required components: compressor, accumulator tank(s), regulator, two
  pressure gauges (one upstream of the regulator, one downstream),
  pressure relief valve set to 125 psi, vent valve (a manual ball valve
  reachable from the outside to drain the system pre-power-off).
- CONTROL HUB: REV Pneumatic Hub (PH) or CTRE Pneumatics Control
  Module (PCM). Controls solenoids and reads the pressure switch.
- PRESSURE SWITCH: tells the controller when to enable the compressor.
- SOLENOIDS: usually 24 V or 12 V, single- or double-acting.
- CYLINDERS: Bimba flat or round-body cylinders, ~1–2" bore typical.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. DRIVETRAINS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

TANK / WEST-COAST DRIVE:
- 4 or 6 wheels on each side, all driven, often with the center wheel
  dropped ~1/8" for easier turning ("drop center").
- Pros: simple, robust, low cost, predictable.
- Cons: no strafing (lateral motion).

MECANUM:
- 4 wheels with diagonal rollers; combining wheel directions enables
  strafing and rotation.
- Pros: omnidirectional, low part count.
- Cons: less efficient, weaker pushing power, less popular at top level.

SWERVE:
- Each wheel is on its own steering module with two motors (one for
  drive, one for azimuth/turn).
- Off-the-shelf modules: SDS MK4/MK4i/MK4n, WCP SwerveX, REV MAXSwerve,
  AndyMark Swerve & Steer.
- Pros: full omnidirectional control, fast, dominant in modern FRC.
- Cons: cost, complexity, more programming, more failure modes.

GEAR RATIOS (drivetrain): top-end speed typically 12–18 ft/s adjusted
for the year's game. Lower speed = more pushing power; higher = faster.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. ROBOT SUBSYSTEMS (TYPICAL ROLES)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- INTAKE: collects game pieces from the floor or a station. Common
  styles: over-the-bumper roller, under-the-bumper roller, hooded
  intake, claw, mecanum/squish-wheel.
- INDEXER / FEEDER: stores or queues game pieces inside the robot
  between intake and scoring mechanism.
- SHOOTER / LAUNCHER: flywheel-based or catapult/punch mechanism for
  launching game pieces toward a goal.
- ARM / ELEVATOR: extends the scoring mechanism to a specific height
  or position. Often combined: an elevator with an arm on the
  end-effector.
- ENDGAME MECHANISM: hooks, climbers, winches that engage a field
  element at the end of the match for bonus points.
- VISION: a Limelight, PhotonVision-running co-processor (Orange Pi /
  Raspberry Pi), or a USB webcam used for AprilTag pose estimation,
  game-piece detection, or auto-aim.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SENSORS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- ENCODERS: count rotations of a shaft. Integrated in NEO/Kraken/Falcon;
  external for through-bore (CTRE Mag, REV Through-Bore, CANcoder).
- LIMIT SWITCHES: mechanical or magnetic (Hall-effect) sensors to detect
  end-of-travel on elevators, arms, intakes.
- PIGEON 2 / NavX: IMU (gyro + accelerometer) for heading and balance.
- BEAM-BREAK SENSORS (photo-eye, Banner sensor): detect a game piece
  passing through an indexer.
- TIME-OF-FLIGHT SENSORS (e.g. PlayingWithFusion CANrange, Rev 2m
  distance sensor): distance to a wall or game piece.
- LIMELIGHT 2+/3/3G/4: vision coprocessor for retroreflective tape (old),
  AprilTag pose, and ML-based game-piece detection.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
12. PROGRAMMING
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- WPILib is the official robot framework, available in Java, C++, and
  Python (RobotPy). Java is by far the most common in FRC.
- The robot code is built with VS Code + the WPILib extension and
  deployed to the RoboRIO over the network.
- ROBOT MODES: Disabled, Autonomous, Teleop, Test. Each has a periodic
  function that runs every 20 ms.
- COMMAND-BASED PROGRAMMING is the recommended paradigm:
    * Subsystems wrap hardware (Drivetrain, Intake, Shooter).
    * Commands are units of behavior (drive forward, run intake until
      beam-break, score). Commands can be sequenced, paralleled,
      conditionalised, and composed.
    * Triggers/Buttons bind commands to inputs.
- DRIVER STATION: NI/FIRST app on a Windows laptop. Connects to the
  radio over wired Ethernet at events. Sends enable signals,
  joystick/controller inputs, and receives diagnostics + battery
  voltage + match time.
- COMMON LIBRARIES: PathPlanner / Choreo (trajectory-following auto),
  YAGSL (swerve), PhotonLib, Limelight Lib, ReduxLib, CTRE Phoenix 6,
  REVLib.
- AUTONOMOUS routines typically follow pre-generated paths and trigger
  scoring actions at waypoints. Modern teams pre-build many short autos
  selected with a SmartDashboard / Shuffleboard / Elastic chooser.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
13. CONTROLS & DRIVER STATION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- Most drive teams use Xbox controllers, Logitech F310s, or custom
  button boards. Some use thrustmasters / flight sticks.
- A drive team is up to 5 people: DRIVER, OPERATOR, HUMAN PLAYER,
  COACH, and TECHNICIAN. The coach is the only one allowed to talk to
  the other alliance partners outside of brief moments.
- At events, robots connect to the FIELD MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (FMS) over
  the venue's wired field network. Practice connections use a home
  radio in standalone mode.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
14. AWARDS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

JUDGED (off-field, narrative):
- IMPACT AWARD (formerly the CHAIRMAN'S AWARD): the most prestigious
  award in FRC. Recognises the team that best embodies the mission of
  FIRST and creates the broadest impact on STEM in their community.
  Winning Impact at any event qualifies the team for Worlds.
- ENGINEERING INSPIRATION: second to Impact in judging; recognises
  outreach + a strong engineering program.
- DEAN'S LIST: 1–2 high-school students per event recognised for
  outstanding leadership and effect on their team's growth.
- WOODIE FLOWERS AWARD: recognises an outstanding adult mentor.
- ROOKIE ALL-STAR / ROOKIE INSPIRATION: for first-year teams.
- SUB-JUDGED AWARDS rotate: Innovation in Control, Industrial Design,
  Quality, Creativity, Excellence in Engineering, Imagery, Team
  Spirit, Gracious Professionalism, Safety.

ROBOT-PERFORMANCE:
- WINNERS of the event (the winning alliance — 3 or 4 teams).
- FINALISTS.
- HIGHEST ROOKIE SEED / HIGHEST ROOKIE FINISH.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
15. CORE VALUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

FIRST has two flagship cultural concepts:
- GRACIOUS PROFESSIONALISM (Woodie Flowers): "fierce competition and
  mutual gain are not separate notions." Compete hard, share knowledge,
  help your opponents.
- COOPERTITION: cooperation and competition together; usually rewarded
  by a yearly co-op ranking-point mechanic.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
16. TEAM ROLES & SUB-TEAMS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Most FRC teams organise into sub-teams that mirror engineering
disciplines:

- MECHANICAL / FABRICATION: machining, fabricating, assembling. Owns
  the mill, lathe, CNC, drill press, hand tools.
- ELECTRICAL: wiring, crimping, breakers, PDH, RoboRIO, radio, CAN bus,
  pneumatics control.
- PROGRAMMING / SOFTWARE: WPILib robot code, autonomous, vision,
  driver-station tooling, simulation.
- CAD / DESIGN: Onshape (most common in FRC), Fusion 360, SolidWorks,
  Inventor. CAD release process gates manufacturing.
- DRIVE TEAM: see §13.
- STRATEGY / SCOUTING: pre-event game analysis, in-event scouting,
  pick-list construction for alliance selection.
- BUSINESS / OUTREACH: sponsorship, finances, awards submissions,
  outreach events, brand/social media.
- SAFETY CAPTAIN: required at events; runs safety check-ins and
  represents the team to the Safety Advisor.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
17. SHOP & SAFETY ESSENTIALS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- Always wear ANSI Z87.1+ safety glasses in the shop. Closed-toe shoes.
- Tie back long hair, no loose sleeves or jewellery near rotating
  machinery.
- Hearing protection near the CNC mill, router, and any prolonged
  power-tool use.
- Lock-out / tag-out before working on the robot's electrical system:
  unplug the battery and inspect the main breaker.
- Pneumatics: ALWAYS vent the system to 0 psi before opening any fitting.
  Verify with the downstream gauge.
- Lifting the robot: use the cart or two people. The 125-lb robot is
  awkward and back-injury prone.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
18. COMMON FRC TOOLS & PROCESSES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- CAD-FIRST DESIGN: model the robot in Onshape before fabricating.
  Releases gate cuts.
- 3D PRINTING: PETG, PLA+, ABS, and ONYX (Markforged) for structural
  prints. Wheels, intake rollers, hooded shapes are commonly printed.
- LASER CUTTING: 1/8" or 1/4" polycarbonate for guards and panels.
- WATERJET / CNC ROUTER: aluminum (6061-T6) plates for structural
  parts.
- MILL & LATHE: shafts, spacers, custom fittings.
- BANDSAW, DRILL PRESS, BELT SANDER: weekly-use tools.
- HARDWARE STANDARDS: most FRC fasteners are 8-32, 10-32, 1/4-20.
  Metric is used for some COTS gearboxes (M3, M5) and bearings (5mm,
  6mm bore IDs).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
19. COMMON PITFALLS & TROUBLESHOOTING (THINGS THE AI CAN PROBE)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- Robot won't enable: check the radio status LEDs, RoboRIO comms light,
  and Driver Station status. Usually a network or battery-voltage issue.
- Motor doesn't spin: check CAN ID, breaker, wire seating in the PDH,
  and that the motor controller's status light is green (not orange/red).
- Code deploys but motor jitters: PWM/encoder direction mismatch, PID
  gains too high, or a damaged encoder cable.
- Pneumatics won't actuate: compressor not running (check pressure
  switch), solenoid wired backward, or system never reached 60 psi.
- Drivetrain pulls to one side: encoder mis-scaling, gyro drift, or a
  bound wheel/gearbox.
- Brownout: battery is too discharged, draw is too high, or the main
  breaker is heat-tripping. Swap battery, check current limits.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
21. GLOSSARY (HIGH-FREQUENCY ACRONYMS)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- FRC: FIRST Robotics Competition.
- FIRST: For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology.
- WPILib: the official FRC software framework.
- RoboRIO: the FRC robot computer.
- PDH/PDP: Power Distribution Hub / Panel.
- VRM/RPM: Voltage Regulator Module / Radio Power Module.
- RSL: Robot Signal Light.
- FMS: Field Management System.
- DS: Driver Station.
- COTS: Commercial Off-The-Shelf (parts).
- CAN: Controller Area Network (the robot's main motor-controller bus).
- IMU: Inertial Measurement Unit (gyro + accelerometer).
- CIM, NEO, Kraken, Falcon: motor model names.
- RP: Ranking Point.
- DCMP: District Championship.
- IRI: Indiana Robotics Invitational (premier off-season event).
- Chezy Champs: Bay Area off-season event hosted by 254.
- Battlecry: New England off-season event.
- Impact (formerly Chairman's): top judged award.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
22. WHAT THE AI SHOULD AVOID
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

- Don't quote scoring values, ranking-point thresholds, or field
  dimensions for THIS year unless they're stated in the file (they
  change annually).
- Don't invent specific team accomplishments or year-by-year facts.
- When unsure, ask the student a conceptual question (subsystems,
  electrical, programming, safety, FRC culture) rather than a
  year-specific trivia question.
