VERTICAL MILLING MACHINE OPERATION
Mentor Knowledge Guide — FIRST Robotics Team

SAFETY REQUIREMENTS
Safety glasses are mandatory — actual ANSI-rated eyewear, not sunglasses. Tie back long hair; a spinning end mill grabs it without warning. Remove all loose jewelry. Wear close-toed shoes and no loose sleeves. If something feels wrong mid-cut, hit the E-stop and ask a mentor.

MACHINE OVERVIEW
A vertical milling machine holds a rotating cutting tool (the spindle) above a workpiece mounted on a movable table. The table moves in X (left/right), Y (front/back), and Z (up/down) axes using handwheels or power feed. The quill is a separate Z-axis on the head that allows quick plunge moves without repositioning the knee. The spindle speed is set via belts or a variable-speed dial depending on the machine.

SETUP — SECURING THE WORKPIECE
Mount the vise and tighten it with T-bolts. Indicate the jaw using a dial test indicator — tap until runout is under 0.002". Place stock on parallels in the vise and seat it with a dead-blow mallet. Zero your axes with an edge finder: touch off each edge, split the difference, and set your DRO to zero.

TOOL SELECTION — END MILLS
For aluminum, use uncoated or ZrN-coated 2-flute end mills — the extra flute gullet clears chips better. For steel, switch to 4-flute TiN or TiAlN-coated end mills. Size matters: a larger diameter end mill is stiffer and handles heavier cuts; a smaller one fits tighter pockets. Never use a dull tool — a dull end mill pushes instead of cuts and causes chatter or tool pull-out.

SPEEDS AND FEEDS
RPM = (SFM x 3.82) / tool diameter

Typical SFM values:
- 6061 aluminum: 300–600 SFM
- Mild steel (1018): 80–120 SFM
- Stainless steel (304): 50–80 SFM

Feed rate (IPM) = RPM x chip load x number of flutes. Start conservative and increase until you get clean, curling chips, not dust or blue smoke.

COMMON OPERATIONS
Facing: Use a fly cutter or face mill to flatten a surface. Take light passes (0.05") at a high step-over.
Slotting: Full-width cut equal to tool diameter. Reduce your feed rate by 30–50% — this is the hardest cut for the tool.
Profiling: Side-milling with 25–50% radial engagement. Climb milling gives a better finish on aluminum.
Drilling: Center drill first, then drill to size. A mill is more accurate than a drill press for hole location.

DEPTH OF CUT
Roughing passes: up to 1x tool diameter axially, 50% radially. Leave 0.010–0.020" for finish.
Finishing passes: 0.005–0.010" depth, full or near-full axial depth of the finished wall.

CHIP EVACUATION AND COOLANT
Aluminum chips pack fast. Use an air blast or WD-40 mist to clear chips from the cut. Never let chips re-cut — this dulls tools and mars the surface. For steel, use cutting oil on the tool and workpiece.

SHUTDOWN AND CLEANUP
Return the table to center. Power off the spindle and wait for it to fully stop before reaching near it. Remove and store tools in a holder, never loose on the table. Brush chips into the chip pan — do not use your hands. Wipe down the table and vise with a lightly oiled rag.

COMMON MISTAKES AND CHATTER
Chatter is a rattling or squealing that leaves a wavy pattern on the surface. Causes: tool sticking out too far, loose vise, wrong spindle speed, or too aggressive a feed. Fix it by shortening tool stick-out, adjusting RPM, or reducing depth of cut. Stop and diagnose before continuing — don't push through it.
