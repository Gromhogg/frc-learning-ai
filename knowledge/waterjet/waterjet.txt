SAFETY REQUIREMENTS
The waterjet stream at operating pressure (60,000+ PSI) will cut through a hand instantly — treat it like a live blade at all times. Never reach into the cutting zone while the pump is running. Keep bystanders 3 feet back from the tank. Wear safety glasses to protect against splatter. Hearing protection is recommended — the cutting stream is loud. Never point the nozzle at anything you don't intend to cut. When the machine finishes, confirm the high-pressure water is fully off before reaching into the tank.

WHAT IS A WATERJET
A waterjet cutter accelerates water through a small orifice (0.010–0.015") to 60,000 PSI, then mixes it with abrasive garnet sand in a focusing tube. The resulting abrasive stream cuts through almost any material — aluminum, steel, titanium, carbon fiber, glass, foam, rubber — with no heat-affected zone. Because there is no heat, there is no warping, no hardening, and no melted edges. For robotics, the waterjet is the fastest way to get complex flat geometry from sheet aluminum with tight tolerances.

MACHINE OVERVIEW
The machine has a high-pressure pump (intensifier or direct-drive), a cutting head mounted on an XY gantry over a water tank, and an abrasive hopper. The tank is filled with water to contain the stream after it exits the material. A CNC controller moves the cutting head along the programmed path. Tolerances are typically ±0.005" to ±0.010" depending on cut quality setting and material thickness.

MATERIALS AND THICKNESS LIMITS
Aluminum: Up to 4–6" thick is possible; for robotics we typically cut 1/8"–3/8" 6061 plate.
Steel and stainless: Cuts well. Thicker sections require slower speeds.
Titanium: Cuts cleanly — one of the waterjet's advantages over other processes.
Carbon fiber: Excellent — no delamination, no heat, no dust (wet cutting contains fibers).
Polycarbonate / HDPE / acrylic: Cuts perfectly. No melting.
Wood: Works but swells from water — use only when other options are unavailable.
Hardened tool steel or ceramics: Generally too hard; consult mentor.

ABRASIVE (GARNET)
The abrasive is 80-mesh garnet. Abrasive flow rate is set in the software — a typical robotics cut uses 0.5–1.0 lb/min. Running without abrasive (pure waterjet mode) only works for soft materials like foam or rubber. Always confirm the hopper is loaded before starting a cut. Running with an empty hopper damages the focusing tube.

CUT QUALITY SETTINGS
Most waterjet software (OMAX Layout, FlowPath, Hypertherm ProNest) lets you set a quality level from 1 (fastest, rough edge) to 5 (slowest, fine edge). For robotics structural parts we use quality 3–4. Use quality 5 only for bearing bores or precision fit surfaces. Quality 1–2 is fine for prototypes or holes that will be drilled out.

KERF AND TOLERANCES
The waterjet removes about 0.030–0.040" of material (the kerf). Good CAM software compensates for this automatically. For bearing holes, always design undersized and finish-drill or ream to final size — the waterjet's edge has slight taper (wider at entry, narrower at exit) that makes precision bores unreliable.

NESTING PARTS
Arrange parts tightly on your sheet in the CAM software to minimize material waste. Leave at least 0.25" between parts and 0.5" from the sheet edge. Shared cuts (two parts sharing one cut line) save time and material but require more careful CAM setup.

LEAD-IN AND LEAD-OUT
The cutter starts the stream on a lead-in — a short entry cut that begins away from the part edge so any pierce-point irregularity doesn't affect the final geometry. Lead-ins are typically 0.1–0.2" long at an angle or perpendicular to the edge. Set lead-ins in the CAM software; never start a cut directly on a finished edge.

OPERATION WORKFLOW
1. Create or import your DXF in the waterjet CAM software.
2. Assign materials, thickness, and cut quality.
3. Add lead-ins and verify kerf compensation is on.
4. Nest parts on the sheet and check extents against the machine cutting area.
5. Load the sheet onto the machine table with material supports (slats). Clamp or weight if needed.
6. Set XY zero at the front-left corner of your sheet.
7. Set Z height — the cutting head should be 0.10–0.15" above the top surface.
8. Confirm the abrasive hopper has garnet and the pump is warmed up.
9. Run the program. Monitor from a safe distance.
10. After the program ends, confirm high-pressure water is off before retrieving parts.

AFTER CUTTING
Parts will be wet and have garnet on them — rinse with fresh water and dry. Deburr edges with a file or Scotch-Brite. Inspect critical dimensions before assembling. Dispose of wet garnet slurry per your facility's guidelines — it is not hazardous but is heavy and can clog drains.

COMMON PROBLEMS
Taper on hole walls: Normal; minimize by reducing cut speed (higher quality setting). Finish-ream for precision fits.
Striation marks on the cut edge: Feed rate too fast. Reduce speed or increase quality setting.
Part moved during cut: Add bridges or use better fixturing. Thin parts can flip up when nearly cut through.
Abrasive not flowing: Check hopper level, check abrasive line for clogs. Never run a cut without abrasive — it will damage the focusing tube.
